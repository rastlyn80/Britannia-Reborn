# Leviathan breath in native water

Leviathan shares body 77 with Kraken. Its stock body hue is `0x0481`. The client uses that hue and the exact five-frame group 12 cue to select the breath. Kraken and other cast signatures retain their existing cast route. Leviathan's stock moving graphic is `0x36D4` with hue `0x01ED`. The native attack applies a 70% physical and 30% cold damage mix.

The motion uses the existing cast on the original Third Dawn Kraken rig, with a separate neck pullback and release. A point fitted to the front of the original head follows the posed mesh. The effect tint still comes from the native projectile hue. The [Kraken source and terrain record](KRAKENS.md) describes the 28-bone model, original alpha, local art preparation and waterline route. The fitted breath retains the existing swimming and waterline behaviour. A fall can interrupt it.

Classic and Enhanced each passed eight decoded checks, including overlapping melee, the native projectile hue, both body 77 cast routes and the static corpse hue. A Forward+ render confirmed the anchor at the front lip. In the disposable native trial, stock Leviathan AI fired from actual wet terrain in both profiles. The bolts launched at 1,300 and 1,304 ms with zero measured muzzle gap. The targets lost 38 and 40 hit points.

The native breath trial placed the creature and its target in an isolated water arena. Separate fishing trials supplied a stock Fabled Fishing Net and placed each disposable test character within casting range of deep sea. Players used the net, responded to the normal targeting cursor, and received 23 splash effects from the native timer. The server consumed the net and spawned body 77, hue `0x0481`, credited to the fisher. Classic and Enhanced passed 13 checks each. The encounters took 17,666 and 17,623 ms, respectively. The native corpse contained both stock ropes and a message in a bottle in each run. A naturally fought kill and the chance-based artifact rewards remain open.

An administrative recolour outside Leviathan's stock hue would require another native identity cue to route the breath.
